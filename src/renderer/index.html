Reply assistant

Learns how you write from your Sent mail, then drafts replies that sound like you. Off until you turn it on.

Step 1

Turn it on and add a key

What leaves your Mac

To write a reply, Letterhead sends the email you are replying to (and the earlier messages in that conversation), a few of your past replies that are similar, and your style notes to the AI provider you choose, using your own API key. Building your style notes sends a sample of up to 45 of your sent emails, once.

Your Sent mail index, the style profile and the reply drafts are stored only on this Mac. Your API key is encrypted with your macOS Keychain.

Google's Gemini API terms say that with a free key, Google uses what you send to improve its products, and human reviewers may read it after it is disconnected from your account. With billing turned on, it does not. For anything private, write that reply yourself.

Getting a free Gemini key, about a minute
  1. Open aistudio.google.com/app/apikey and sign in with your Google account.
  2. Click Create API key, then copy it.
  3. Paste it above and press Save, then Test.
Step 2

Learn your style

Letterhead reads your newest sent emails, keeps the part you wrote (not the quoted email below it), and measures your greetings, sign-offs, length and favorite phrases on this Mac. Then it asks the AI to describe your tone from a small sample.

Automatic replies

Never replies to no-reply addresses, mailing lists and newsletters, automated or bulk mail, out-of-office messages, emails sent to more than 5 people, mail older than a day, or the same person twice in 24 hours. It also stops if you reply yourself first.

More options
Gmail setup, about two minutes

Make an App Password

  1. Turn on 2-Step Verification for your Google Account if it is not on yet. Google only offers App Passwords with it enabled.
  2. Open myaccount.google.com/apppasswords and sign in.
  3. Type a name like Letterhead and click Create.
  4. Copy the 16 character code into the App password box here. Spaces are fine.

If the page says the setting is unavailable, your account may be managed by a school or work admin who has turned App Passwords off. A personal Gmail account works best.

iCloud setup

Make an app-specific password

  1. Sign in at account.apple.com.
  2. Under Sign-In and Security, choose App-Specific Passwords and create one named Letterhead.
  3. Paste it here. Use your iCloud address as the email.
Any SMTP server

Your provider's outgoing (SMTP) server name, port, and the password it expects. Many providers need an app password instead of your normal one. Their help pages list these settings.

Help

If something goes wrong

Letterhead keeps a log of errors on this Mac. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Copy diagnostics puts a short report on your clipboard (versions, settings, recent errors) to paste into a message. It leaves out passwords, keys and email text.
Why a cap

Sending limits

Google's help pages list a limit of 500 emails a day for personal Gmail accounts and 2,000 for Workspace accounts, counted over a rolling 24 hours. Going past it can block sending for up to a day.

A draft addressed to three people counts as three here, to stay on the safe side.

This counter only sees mail sent from Letterhead. Mail you send from Gmail itself counts toward Google's limit too, so leave some room.

For cold outreach, an interval of a minute or more tends to read as ordinary sending and gives you time to catch a typo before it reaches everyone.

Scheduled batches share this cap. If a batch reaches it, that batch pauses and picks up again by itself once there is room.
